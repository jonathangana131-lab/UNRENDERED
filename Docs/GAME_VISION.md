# UNRENDERED — Game Vision

## Core pitch

UNRENDERED is a persistent multiplayer physics-horror universe in which reality is only fully resolved where observation forces it to become true.

It should feel less like a game level and more like a glitch in the universe: a dream reconstructing human interiors from incomplete memories.

## What a session feels like

A player enters alone or with an Entry Cohort of friends. The opening space is believable and often quiet. They learn by touching the world: doors have mass, furniture moves, lights and circuits behave, radios and cameras are physical devices, and the first-person camera belongs to a body.

Exploration produces questions rather than quest markers:
- footsteps that may be another player, an entity, or fictive history,
- a phone ringing somewhere physically reachable,
- a couch remembered from an earlier region but reconstructed incorrectly,
- a room with impossible daylight,
- moved furniture from a real player,
- a security feed showing a place that does not fit nearby topology,
- one tiny architectural error in an otherwise perfect office,
- a Still Life that is not obviously alive.

Atmosphere can change meaningfully within seconds or minutes. There are no required hour-long waits for the world to become interesting and no generic `LEVEL 2` transition.

## Long-term play

Progression is primarily knowledge, relationships, equipment, recordings, maps, stable routes, and player-created reality anchors—not XP levels.

Players can:
- explore effectively infinite space,
- document anomalies,
- locate real people by voice/radio/phone/evidence,
- establish temporary settlements from physical found objects,
- stabilize important regions through observation/anchors,
- leave traces other people discover,
- recover old cameras and recordings,
- catalogue entities without perfect certainty,
- search for rare persistent anomalies and the unresolved concept called **The Boundary**.

## The universe

The world does not merely randomize rooms. It invents apparent purpose and fake history: construction era, renovations, maintenance, leaks, wear, electrical topology, furniture culture, and past incidents. Normality is generated first; wrongness is a restrained second pass.

The universe appears to understand concepts such as office, school, hotel, waiting room, home, retail, institutional corridor, maintenance space, recreation center, warehouse, and flooded interior—but it does not fully understand why humans build or inhabit them.

This produces dream logic:
- a school corridor gradually inherits hotel carpet,
- an office ceiling grid continues through a bedroom,
- a tiny doorway opens into a megaspace,
- a hallway looks 20 m long but takes much longer to traverse,
- a remembered room returns with one impossible proportion,
- a safe-looking lounge has windows showing a sunset that never changes.

## Horror rules

1. Fear is not constant.
2. Normal spaces must be convincing enough to make violations matter.
3. Do not replace atmosphere with glitch overlays.
4. Do not spawn an enemy behind the player because pacing says it is time.
5. Entities exist within the world rather than for the player.
6. Still Lifes are scene compositions, not a list of monster prefabs.
7. Silence, beauty, nostalgia, comfort, and boredom are usable horror ingredients.
8. The world may provide conflicting evidence about what it is; do not over-explain the lore early.

## Multiplayer

Everyone conceptually occupies one WorldId, although Roblox server processes host active pieces of it. Most people are far apart. A genuine encounter should feel significant.

There are no default floating nameplates or global join spam. Communication favors proximity voice, physical radios, phones, intercoms, writing, recordings, and signs. Safety controls remain conventional and accessible.

Unobserved space between distant people does not need to exist as stored geometry. As their observed bubbles approach, a Reality Reconciliation system creates a canonical bridge that preserves both resolved histories.

## Player-created culture

Objects and places can gain significance. A random blue chair can become a navigation landmark, then a settlement marker, then a famous community location. High-significance/anchored truths become increasingly resistant to forgetting.

Abandoned settlements can be discovered after surrounding low-confidence architecture has changed, creating emergent archaeology no scripted narrative can fully reproduce.

## Name

The title is **UNRENDERED** because the technical and fictional rule are the same: beyond observation, reality is not necessarily rendered, instantiated, or even fully decided.
